# EV and Plug-in Hybrid Buyer Guide (P-410)

Use this guide when a customer is considering an electric vehicle or a plug-in hybrid. Most EV questions come down to charging, winter range and incentives.

## Home charging

Most owners charge at home overnight on a Level 2 (240 V) charger. Installation usually needs a dedicated 40 A circuit. Northgate partners with Brightline Electric, who give our customers a free quote within 5 business days and a 10% discount on installation. A standard Level 1 cable (regular household outlet) is included with every EV we sell, but it only adds about 6 to 8 km of range per hour.

Customers who park on the street or in a shared garage without charging should usually be steered toward a hybrid or plug-in hybrid instead.

## Winter range

Expect 20% to 30% less range in a Canadian winter, and up to 40% on very cold days below minus 20°C. Two habits help: preconditioning the cabin and battery while the car is still plugged in, and using the heated seats and steering wheel rather than heating the whole cabin. Vehicles with a heat pump (Ioniq 5, EV9, Model Y) lose less range than those without.

## Incentives

The federal iZEV rebate program has changed several times. Always check the current federal eligibility on the Transport Canada website before mentioning a rebate, and never include a rebate in a written quote until it is confirmed. Ontario does not currently offer a provincial EV purchase rebate.

## Battery warranty

High-voltage batteries are covered by the manufacturer for 8 years or 160,000 km against failure and excessive capacity loss.

## Plug-in hybrids

Plug-in hybrids such as the Kia Sorento PHEV give roughly 50 km of electric driving before the gas engine takes over, which covers most daily commutes. They are a good fit for customers who want to try electric driving but take regular long trips.
